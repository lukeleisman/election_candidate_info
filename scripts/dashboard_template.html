What the columns mean, and what is left out
  • Cash on hand: funds available at the end of each committee's latest report, added across the candidate's own committees. A committee whose latest report is older is flagged "cash not current".
  • Raised: individual gifts (itemized and not), transfers from other committees and other receipts, on reports wholly inside the window. Loans and in-kind gifts are separate. Transfers between a candidate's own matched committees are not counted; transfers from their committees for other offices are (open question).
  • $1,000+ gifts after the last quarterly: A-1 reports filed since; not in Raised yet.
  • Itemized donors: distinct donors of itemized gifts (over $150), people and organizations, by last name + first name + zip. Two other identity rules are in data/tables/donor_mix.csv.
  • Small gifts share: individual gifts too small to itemize, as a share of all receipts (raised + loans + in-kind).
  • Top 10 donors' share: dollars from the 10 largest itemized donors, as a share of Raised.
  • Median gift (people): median itemized gift from individuals.
  • From 606 zips: share of itemized donor dollars from zip codes starting 606 (misses 60707, 60827).
  • Matched committee: ballot candidate matched to a State Board of Elections candidate record, then every committee the state links to that record is listed (as Illinois Sunshine does). "Name only" committees carry the candidate's name but the state does not link them. No match can mean no committee yet, or one the matching missed.
  • Sunshine-style total (committee table): our attempt to match the "Funds Raised" figure on Illinois Sunshine's race pages, which counts loans and in-kind gifts and starts at the committee's first report. It matched Sunshine exactly for committees started in 2025-26 (checked Nov 12, 2026). For older committees Sunshine starts at a date in 2023 we could not reproduce, so they show "not comparable".
  • A person who filed twice is listed twice; the second filing is marked and left out of the race figures.
